Detailed Diagnosis

"The visual indicators on the tomato specimen show classic symptoms of late blight. Large, irregular, water-soaked patches are visible on the leaves, which have turned purplish-black. During periods of high humidity, a white, fuzzy growth of the fungus can be observed on the undersides of the leaves."

Late blight is caused by the oomycete Phytophthora infestans. It is highly contagious and spreads through airborne spores. Current humidity levels in your region (84%) are optimal for the pathogen's development. If unchecked, the stems will eventually rot and the fruit will develop firm, dark brown, leathery spots.

Treatment Plan

01

Quarantine Affected Area

Remove and destroy all infected plant material immediately. Do not compost these materials as spores can survive.

02

Fungicide Application

Apply a copper-based fungicide or Chlorothalonil to the remaining healthy plants in a 10-meter radius.

03

Humidity Control

Increase spacing between plants and switch to drip irrigation to keep foliage dry during evening hours.
